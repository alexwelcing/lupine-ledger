Paper · The law

The Projection Law: Model-Ensemble Errors Point at Their Binding Constraint

Working draft · June 2026 · theory, two pre-registered factorial experiments, replication kit

Typeset PDF (PRX) ↓ IMMI format PDF ↓ Replication kit → Internal review (AI-assisted) → Internal adversarial pass →

Abstract (June 2026; see the status note above)

When many independently constructed models agree, the agreement is routinely read as confidence. We formalize and test the opposite reading: a model family is a projection operator, fitting drives every member toward the point of the family's reachable set nearest the truth, and the shared residual — one direction in observable space — is a fingerprint of whatever constraint binds the family. The theory core is stated and checked in Lean 4: normal-cone consensus theorem; participation-ratio gauge; ribbon/consensus decoupling; affine decomposition of closed affine reachable sets; local normal-cone theorem for $C^1$ smooth non-convex immersions; and Hoeffding concentration of the empirical second-moment matrix used by the gauge. We then test the law's sharpest consequence — errors organize by constraint, not by implementation — with pre-registered factorial experiments at three layers of one epistemic stack: classical interatomic potentials, foundation machine-learned potentials (4 architectures × 2 training functionals), and DFT implementations (12 methods, 384 crystals). A complete 3×3×3 supercell benchmark on 16 cubic metals shows that one bias direction per functional transfers out of sample; when the held-out reference sets how far to correct, the foundation-MLIP mean MAE falls from 17.84 GPa to 10.36 GPa, a ceiling rather than a deployable correction [revised 2026-11-11]. Each experiment carried an explicit refutation condition; none was triggered, and four of seven registered predictions failed and are reported as failures. The anisotropy persists at every layer — median participation ratio 1.09, ~1.3, and 1.10 out of 3 — while its direction rotates to the next constraint upstream.

Paper · The benchmark

Layer-2: A Sub-Core-Hour 3×3×3 Elastic-Constant Reference Benchmark for MatPES MLIPs

Working draft · June 2026, revised · 16 cubic metals, 4 foundation potentials, 2 functionals, 128 cases

Read in the Library → Summary JSON →

Abstract (revised 2026-11-11, from the Library edition)

We report a 128-case 3×3×3 elastic-constant reference matrix for four MatPES foundation machine-learned interatomic potential (MLIP) labels on 16 cubic metals, against PBE and approximate r2SCAN targets (scalar bulk-modulus shifts of PBE). The matrix costs less than one CPU core-hour; raw predictions have a mean Cij MAE of 17.8 GPa. One bias direction per functional, fitted leave-one-out, transfers out of sample. When the held-out reference sets how far to correct along that direction, the mean MAE falls to 10.4 GPa (PBE 15.0 → 9.4 GPa; r2SCAN 20.7 → 11.3 GPa). Because the held-out target sets the coefficient, this is an oracle ceiling, not a deployable correction, and it cannot increase a held-out residual by construction. A correction that works without seeing the answer is still open. Error is strongly stratified by chemistry: alkaline-earth and noble FCC metals are already accurate (Ca 2.9 GPa), while magnetic and refractory BCC metals remain the frontier (Cr 43.5 GPa).

Forty Years of Interatomic Potentials, One Error Geometry

Hyper-Ribbon Universality and Why Pooled Benchmarks Mislead · Working draft · June 2026 · archived 2026-11-11

Archived 11 November 2026. Kept for the record; not for citation. The draft's central claim, a universal low-dimensional error geometry across “559 potentials”, is no longer supported: the 559 are model–element tensors with 423 distinct KIM ids, the historical counts are not re-verified, low participation ratios are partly rank-limited, and the hyper-ribbon claim is Open and descriptive. The crystal-structure (BCC/FCC) pattern it mentions was 1.5% data contamination. The draft’s I² = 98.6% and random-effects intervals were computed on dependent C11/C12/C44 rows of the same tensors; they are anti-conservative and no longer reported as inference. Current status: Hypothesis Ledger.

Abstract (June 2026, superseded)

Prediction errors are not noise. Across 559 interatomic potentials and forty years of development — pair potentials through embedded-atom methods to modern functional forms — elastic-constant errors universally occupy low-dimensional hyper-ribbon manifolds (effective dimensionality 1.0–2.3 out of 3, median 1.09), and the geometry has not moved while accuracy improved: the sloppy-model universality class, observed for the first time in cross-model error space rather than single-model parameter space. The methodological consequence is sharp: between-element heterogeneity is extreme (I² = 98.6%), pooled benchmarks misstate model accuracy (within-family correlation 0.95 vs 0.82 pooled), and the paper demonstrates the repair — stratified evaluation with random-effects meta-analysis. The paper also audits itself: an apparent element-level electronic-structure signal is traced to a fitting-depth confounder, and a Born-screened extension to three foundation MLIPs (with mechanical-stability failures reported, not hidden) provides the proof-of-concept the companion law paper develops. Crystal-structure-dependent correlation patterns are reported among the empirical findings.

Provenance & verification

All three papers are working drafts: not peer-reviewed and not accepted by any venue; the third is archived. Every headline statistic is verifiable from committed raw data: the replication kit (commit-versioned, public) runs Tier 1 verification with NumPy alone in seconds (python tier1_analyze.py) and re-derives the raw elastic constants from public model checkpoints in Tier 2. The 3×3×3 MatPES benchmark is reproducible from lupine/data/aggregate_layer2.py and the raw Cloud Run outputs; the corrected MAEs are also generated by atlas-distill mlip-correct. Pre-registrations were committed before execution (dffbe595, ebf39e33); the round-2 registration with symmetric kill conditions is included. The theory core — normal-cone consensus theorem, participation-ratio gauge, ribbon/consensus decoupling, affine decomposition, smooth non-convex local normal cone, and finite-sample concentration — is stated in Lean 4 in the open repository.
